        METHODS AND INTERPRETATION

        How commercial real estate financing capacity is determined

        Debt capacity is often limited by the most restrictive of three underwriting measures: debt service coverage ratio (DSCR), loan-to-value (LTV), and minimum debt yield. A favorable appraisal does not eliminate a refinance shortfall when NOI or scheduled debt service constrains loan proceeds. The assumptions entered here are illustrative and not verified lender criteria.

        Debt Capacity

        Compare the DSCR, LTV and debt-yield loan ceilings to identify which assumption controls the estimated senior debt amount.

        Refinance Intelligence

        Add existing payoff, financing expenses and reserves to determine the total capital requirement. The difference between that requirement and supportable senior debt can reveal a cash gap.

        Scenario Lab

        Stress-test NOI and the borrowing rate. Downside scenarios can reveal a different binding constraint and an increase in required sponsor capital.

        Capital Structure

        Illustrate junior debt, preferred equity and cash equity. Actual lenders may restrict subordinate capital, and a modeled funding source is not a commitment.

        How investment return and LP/GP distribution estimates work

        Investment returns use year-end annual cash flows, monthly debt amortization and a forward-year NOI exit capitalization. IRR is periodic annual IRR, not XIRR. Calculations exclude income taxes and unmodeled capital expenditures. The equity waterfall illustrates preferred return, return of contributed capital and residual allocation; it does not model catch-up, clawback, multiple investor classes or negotiated legal terms.

        A worked example: why a commercial refinance can leave a cash gap

        Consider an illustrative property valued at $8,000,000, producing $520,000 in annual net operating income (NOI), with a $5,600,000 existing loan payoff and $180,000 in estimated costs and reserves. Assume a 7.50% interest rate, 25-year amortization, minimum DSCR of 1.25x, maximum LTV of 65%, and minimum debt yield of 9%.

        The DSCR test supports approximately $4,691,080 in senior debt; the LTV test supports $5,200,000; and the debt-yield test supports $5,777,778. The lowest limit controls, so estimated senior debt is $4,691,080. Against total refinance uses of $5,780,000, that leaves an illustrative funding gap of $1,088,920. Raising collateral value alone would not resolve the income-driven constraint at these assumptions.

        These are scenario calculations, not lender quotations. Actual lender sizing, property underwriting, borrower credit, reserves, closing costs and capital-source availability may differ.

        Frequently asked questions

        Can a higher property appraisal resolve a refinancing gap?

        Not if cash flow or debt yield is the binding limit. An increase in collateral value alone may have no effect on the maximum senior debt.

        Is a modeled capital stack an approved loan?

        No. Real financing depends on lender-specific underwriting, restrictions, due diligence, borrower qualifications and capital availability.

        What is the difference between amortization and maturity?

        Amortization determines scheduled principal repayment while loan maturity is when the remaining balance must be repaid. A balloon payment may remain.

        Are financial inputs sent to Leva?

        Core calculations run locally in the browser without uploading entered financing assumptions. Separate voluntary inquiries elsewhere on the website have different data-handling terms.
